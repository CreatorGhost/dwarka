DWARKA — Story CandidatesMahabharat-era web action game · hackathon MVP · Black Myth: Wukong structure (cold open → missions with mini-bosses → final boss)

Every candidate below follows the Wukong rule: the opening shows what actually happened — the legend falls, someone the hero loves is broken — and only then does the player inherit the fight. No god is ever playable or killable.

Story A · Recommended

VRISHAKETU — The Last Arrow of the Sun

The only surviving son of Karna is raised by the men who killed his father. When raiders take the last of his people while every warrior rides behind the sacrificial horse, the boy takes the road the horse takes — because it is the same road the raiders took.

LOW sensitivity   Karna-adjacent — treat with reverence

At a glance

Player
Vrishaketu, 17, mortal archer
Source
Jaiminiya Ashvamedha Parva (12th-c. retelling)
Mentor
Arjuna — his father's killer
Villain
Bhishana, rakshasa son of Bakasura
Wukong parallel
Play Karna's last stand and lose (Wukong vs Erlang) → play the successor

Cold open — "The Wheel" (playable, unwinnable, ~3 min)

Kurukshetra, Day 17. You are Karna. Not Vrishaketu — Karna. The sun is low. Your chariot wheel sinks into blood-soaked earth. The player gets the full kit for three minutes: the Vijaya bow, the sun-bright armour that is no longer there. Arjuna's arrows come. You fight. It does not matter how well the player plays — the wheel will not lift, the mantra will not come (Parashurama's curse), and the last screen is Karna stepping down from the chariot to free the wheel with his bare hands, asking for the pause the rules of war promise him. A voice off-screen answers: "Where was dharma when…" The arrow is not shown. Cut to white.

Why this works: it is exactly how Wukong opens — you play the legend and are made to lose — and it is scripture-accurate to the arrow. Karna is beloved, not worshipped; a reverent, unwinnable last stand honours him rather than reducing him to a boss.

Why he fights — the inciting incident

Scene 1 · Hastinapura, after the war. Kunti tells her sons that the man they killed was their brother. Arjuna cannot meet the boy's eyes. Vrishaketu is given his father's bow-hand ring and the name-right to Anga. He asks for nothing. He does not call Arjuna anything.
Scene 2 · The Suta quarter, the night before the horse is loosed. The army is at the yajna ground. Nobody guards the charioteers' streets — Karna's people. Rakshasa raiders under the shape-shifter Lambodari strike. Vrishaketu comes home to find Chitra — the ten-year-old foster-brother who wore a paper sun-crest and called himself "Karna's second archer" — broken on the doorstep, and the rest of the household gone. Chitra's last words: "They asked for you by name."
Scene 3 · The empty court. Yudhishthira has no men to spare: every warrior rides with the horse for a year. A counsel from off-screen (Krishna's voice; he is never shown fighting): "The horse wanders where it wills. Watch where it wills to go." The horse's road and the raiders' trail point the same way. Vrishaketu takes the oath to guard the horse — for Chitra, not for the Pandavas.
The villain's motive (revealed in Mission 5). Bhishana is the son of Bakasura, whom Bhima killed at Ekachakra. Bhima's line is beyond his reach. But Karna's son — raised by the men who killed his father — is a wound he can pry open. He took Chitra as bait to bring Vrishaketu, alive and angry, into his grove; and he wants the horse captured so Arjuna's whole force walks into Vriksha, where at dusk the flowers open into humans and the rakshasas feed.

Story B · Grok's recommendation

EMBERBORN — The Forest That Was Burned

A nameless Naga-blooded child survives the burning of Khandava. Years later, a cousin gathers the survivors around the last cooled ember and Maya's shard to forge a curse against the Kuru line. The Emberborn walks the roads the Pandavas walk — and refuses the weapon.

LOWEST sensitivity   No name recognition

At a glance

Player
Original character, Nishada-Naga youth
Source
Adi Parva (Khandava-daha) as backdrop only
Patron
Maya, the asura architect Arjuna spared
Villain
The Ember King — original, the hero's cousin
Wukong parallel
Nameless successor walks through a legend he was never part of

Cold open — "Fifteen Days of Fire" (playable escape, ~3 min)

Khandava forest, the day Agni came. You are eight. The sky is on fire; Indra's rain turns to steam before it lands; two chariots ring the forest and shoot down everything that flies out. You do not fight — you run: through collapsing canopy, past Nagas coiling into the river, while your mother carries your little sister. At the tree-line an arrow takes your mother. She pushes you both under the roots. The sister's hand slips in the smoke. Cut to black. (Canon: six creatures survived Khandava. Ours is the seventh nobody counted.)

Why this works: the player is on the receiving end of the epic's most morally uncomfortable episode, without any Pandava being a "villain" — Arjuna and Krishna are silhouettes on chariots, never named on screen in the prologue.

Why he fights — the inciting incident

Ten years later. The remnant lives in the cinders. The cousin — older, charismatic, the one who pulled the hero from the roots — has recovered a cooled ember of Agni's fire and a shard of Maya's craft, and is building a curse-weapon: "They burned a forest to build a city. We will burn the city." He needs a child of pure Naga blood to bind the ember. He takes the hero's sister — the one whose hand slipped — now found alive in a Nishada village. She is the binding.
The choice. Maya (NPC patron) tells the Emberborn the truth: the ember will not stop at Indraprastha; a curse on a dynasty burns the dynasty's forests too. Go get your sister; the roads the weapon needs are the roads the Pandavas are walking this year.

Story C · Wildcard

BABHRUVAHANA — The Gem Beneath the World

A prince raised without a father kills a stranger who insulted his kingdom and seized his mother's honour — and learns the stranger was Arjuna, his father. To undo it he must invade the Naga underworld for the life-gem before the stolen heads are lost forever.

MEDIUM — Arjuna dies on screen (canon, but optics)

At a glance

Player
Babhruvahana, prince of Manipur (Arjuna × Chitrangada)
Source
Jaiminiya Ashvamedha — the six-chapter Patala quest
Allies
Ulupi (Naga stepmother), the mongoose army
Villain
Dhritarashtra the Naga elder, whose sons steal the heads
Wukong parallel
Hero's own hand causes the fall; the quest is to undo it

Cold open — "The Horse Enters Manipur" (playable, you WIN, and it is the worst thing that ever happened)

The sacrificial horse strays into Manipur. A stranger kicks the prince in front of his court and calls him "a woman's son who hides behind his mother". The player fights — and wins. Every duel: the boy archer Vrishaketu falls; the giant Bhima flees; then the stranger himself, whose flag bears a monkey. Ganga's whisper hands you the last arrow. Cut on the shot — not on the wound. Then: your mother's scream. The name. Your stepmother's pomegranate tree, dead in the courtyard. You have killed your father, and the boy was your cousin.

Why this works: it inverts Wukong — the legend falls by your hand. The strongest inciting incident of the four. Canon-exact to Jaimini. But it puts Arjuna's death (and Vrishaketu's) inside a player win state — cutscene-only, never a beheading on screen, and even then some fans will hate it.

Why he fights

Ulupi's gem, the Mritasanjivani, can revive them — but it is in Patala, guarded by the Naga council, and the elder Dhritarashtra (namesake of the blind king, friend of the Kauravas) is glad Arjuna is dead. Worse: the heads are gone. Without heads the gem is useless. Babhruvahana has until his mother's fast-unto-death ends.

Story D · The obvious one (and why it is a trap)

ABHIMANYU — The Seventh Gate

Sixteen years old, taught to enter the Chakravyuha in his mother's womb but not to leave it. Seven layers, seven maharathis, one exit that does not exist.

MEDIUM–HIGH — bosses are Drona, Karna, Duryodhana; mandatory death

At a glance

Player
Abhimanyu, Arjuna's son
Source
Drona Parva, Day 13 (core Vyasa canon)
Structure
7 gates = 7 bosses. Perfect level design.
Ending
He dies. There is no other ending.

Cold open — "The Story in the Womb"

Subhadra, pregnant, listens to Arjuna describe how to break the wheel-formation. She falls asleep before the exit. The unborn child hears the first half. Sixteen years later, Day 13: Arjuna has been drawn to the far flank by the Samsaptakas; Drona forms the Chakravyuha; Yudhishthira has no one else. "I know how to go in." "And out?" "…Follow close behind me." Jayadratha shuts the gate behind him.

Verdict: the tightest game structure of the four and the worst fit for the brief — a forced death against the grandfather-figures of the epic is exactly the "hero loses" problem the brief asked us to avoid, just one generation down. Kept here so the comparison is honest.

Shared rules (all stories)

  • No deity or avatar is playable, fightable, or shown losing.
  • Famous canonical kills stay with their canonical killers.
  • Opening card: "Adapted from the Jaiminiya Ashvamedha Parva / regional retellings of the Mahabharata."
  • Classical-register dialogue; no slang (the Adipurush lesson).
  • Rakshasas, cursed beings, hostile kings and original characters are the enemy classes.
